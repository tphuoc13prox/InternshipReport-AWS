Below are my impressions and suggestions after three months in the First Cloud AI Journey program, written in the hope that the program keeps improving for future cohorts.

### Overall Evaluation

**1. Working Environment**  
- The program gives interns room to work on their own: I studied through the FCJ modules and labs, then planned the project and split the work together with my team. It took me some time to get used to this, but I gradually learned to find official AWS documentation, set goals for each week and take full ownership of my part of the work.
- Days at the AWS office let me work directly with my teammates, discuss things faster than over chat, and meet interns from other teams. Events held at the office also gave me the chance to hear from people working in the industry.

**2. Support from Mentor / Team Admin**  
- The mentors and the admin team always gave clear answers whenever our team asked about the requirements of the report, the workshop and the deadlines. Clear notices, such as the one on 23/11 about the shared and personal parts of the report, told us exactly what to prepare and kept us from going in the wrong direction near the end.
- With modules and step-by-step labs, I could learn most of what the project needed on my own. Preparing questions for the mentors also taught me to research first, then describe a problem briefly and with all the information needed.

**3. Relevance of Work to Academic Major**  
- My major is Artificial Intelligence, and my main work in the project was the chatbot on Amazon Bedrock, so it was closely related to what I study. I applied what I had learned about large language models to a real problem: writing prompts, letting the model call tools to fetch data, and evaluating answers with question sets that have clear thresholds.
- Cloud, serverless and infrastructure as code with AWS CDK are areas my university covers little. The project showed me what an AI application needs to run reliably in production: IAM permissions, usage limits, logs, alarms and cost tracking.

**4. Learning & Skill Development Opportunities**  
- What taught me the most was building a project that really runs on AWS with a team of five, from the proposal to deployment and operations. AWS credits and the FCJ labs let me practise on real services, from networking, EC2, S3 and RDS to serverless, CloudFormation, CDK and CloudWatch.
- The five events I attended, from the Buildathon kickoff, AWS Cloud and AI Day and the Fireside Chat with Dr. Werner Vogels to Cloud Mastery 1, helped me keep up with topics that matter right now, such as agentic AI and Amazon Bedrock AgentCore. Besides technical skills, I also got better at working with teammates: splitting work into pull requests, reviewing each other's code and writing bilingual documents.

**5. Company Culture & Team Spirit**  
- The CPAWS team worked well together: everyone had a clear role, every change went through a pull request with cross-review, and we met regularly to agree on direction. When someone got stuck, the others were ready to help, and review comments were always direct but respectful.
- The Smart Plushies for Children volunteering activity showed me Amazon's culture of giving back to the community, and it was also a chance to connect with the staff and other interns outside of work.

**6. Internship Policies / Benefits**  
- Each team has about 200 USD of AWS credits for its project, enough for our team to both experiment and run the system while using only a few dollars. I could also use all FCJ materials and labs and sign up for AWS community events at no cost, so I could practise on real services without worrying about money.
- The check-in and check-out rules for office days are clear, which helped me keep a steady working rhythm and fit the internship around my university schedule.

### Additional Questions

**What did you find most satisfying during your internship?**  
- What satisfied me most was putting my own work into production. The chatbot I was responsible for went from a proposed `POST /chat` contract, through several rounds of benchmarking and prompt tuning, to running on the team's web app and answering users with figures and sourced news.
- I was also glad to work in a team with a clear process, where every decision was recorded and had an owner. It taught me how a software product is built the way companies actually do it.

**What do you think the company should improve for future interns?**  
- I think a suggested learning path for each role in the team would make it easier for new interns to choose which modules and labs to prioritise. For example, the AI members could focus on Bedrock and SageMaker early, while the infrastructure members could go deeper into CDK and monitoring.
- I would also like more networking and sharing sessions between intern teams. Each team solves a different problem, so hearing how other teams designed their architecture and handled difficulties would help everyone learn from each other.

**If recommending to a friend, would you suggest they intern here? Why or why not?**  
- Yes. For friends who want to go into cloud or AI, I think this is a good fit: they practise on real AWS services instead of only theory, ship a working product with a team, and hear directly from AWS engineers.
- I would also tell them in advance that the program relies on self-discipline: nobody hands out daily tasks, so they need to plan their own learning and keep up with their team. Those who are proactive will get a lot out of the program.

### Suggestions & Expectations

**Do you have any suggestions to improve the internship experience?**  
- I suggest offering a path of modules and labs per role (AI/GenAI, platform, data, security) as soon as the teams are formed, so each person knows what to prioritise for their part.
- The program could also collect the common questions about the report and the workshop (template format, shared and personal parts, how to write the worklog) into an FAQ page at the start of the term. Teams could then look things up first, and the admin team would not need to answer the same question many times.

**Would you like to continue this program in the future?**  
- Yes. I hope to keep taking part in AWS and FCAJ activities after the internship, to go deeper into GenAI on AWS, especially Amazon Bedrock Guardrails and Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, which I started exploring at Event 5. I also want to strengthen my machine learning foundation to do better on AI problems in the cloud.

**Any other comments (free sharing):**  
- I sincerely thank the mentors, the admin team and the organizers of the First Cloud AI Journey program for their support and guidance to me and the CPAWS team throughout the three months of the internship. It has been a memorable and valuable experience for me.
